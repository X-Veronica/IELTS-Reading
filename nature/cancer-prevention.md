# An Ounce of Prevention: Managing Global Cancer Risks

SCIENTISTS HAVE long known that some cancers have preventable causes. But reliable estimates of how many, exactly, have been few and far between. In a report published in *Nature Medicine* on February 3rd, 2026, researchers provided the most comprehensive figures to date.

The study covered 30 risk factors, including individual habits like smoking and alcohol consumption, as well as environmental factors such as air pollution and infections. It found that of the nearly 20 million new cancers in 2022, **38% were due to preventable factors**.

### Key Risk Factors
1. **Smoking**: This remains the leading cause of cancers in men globally and for women in America and Europe. It is linked to at least 15 types of cancer.
2. **Infections**: One in ten cancers is caused by an infection. For example, nearly all cervical cancers are caused by the **human papillomavirus (HPV)**, while stomach cancers are mostly linked to the bacterium *Helicobacter pylori*.
3. **Alcohol**: Ranking third overall, it causes 4.6% of all cancers in men.

### Progress and Challenges
Vaccines against HPV and Hepatitis B are now part of routine childhood immunisations in many countries, forecast to prevent millions of future cases. In Britain, cervical cancer rates among women in their 20s have fallen by 90% since the introduction of the HPV jab in 2008.

However, some cancers remain far less amenable to prevention. The onset of breast and pancreatic cancers is mostly due to internal biological mechanisms that scientists have yet to untangle. The hope remains that these, too, may one day join the ranks of cancers with mostly preventable causes.
