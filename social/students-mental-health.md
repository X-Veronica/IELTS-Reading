# Efforts called for on students' mental health
**Source**: China Daily | **Topic**: Social Issues & Education
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐⭐ (Targeting IELTS Band 6.0-7.0)

---

## 📖 Article Content

A recent high-profile case concerning three middle school boys who allegedly killed a classmate has sparked discussions on the mental health of "left-behind" children in China. A seventh-grade student in Handan, Hebei province, was killed on March 10. It is alleged that the suspects, all under 14 years old, had bullied the victim for a long time. Reports indicate that both the victim and the suspects are children of migrant workers.

Zong Chunshan, director of the Beijing Youth Legal and Psychological Counseling Service Center, said left-behind children lack proper family education during critical stages of their development, which may result in **psychological issues** and **behavioral deviations**. 

On March 18, the National Advisory Committee for Students' Mental Health advocated for greater awareness concerning the health of adolescents. The committee stated that the foundation of students' mental health lies in the family, with support from schools and society. However, a 2021 survey showed that some parents still have a limited understanding of depression and feel a strong sense of shame regarding mental illness. They may simplify complex psychological issues as a "lack of interest in studying" or "weak willpower."

Xu Ling, a political adviser, noted that schools often prioritize exam-oriented education over extracurricular activities. Insufficient physical activities have led students to fall into traps of "self-isolation, loneliness, and anxiety." Many parents also have excessive requirements for academic success, leading to severe conflicts. Furthermore, the lack of professional personnel means that mental health services in grassroots institutions need urgent improvement. 

Finally, political adviser Ding Zuohong pointed out that the threshold for the psychological counseling industry is relatively low. He proposed strengthening "frustration education" in schools to teach young students how to effectively manage negative emotions.
